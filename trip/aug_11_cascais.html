Don't-Miss Events on Your Exact Dates

Four experiences that only happen during the week you're there. Plan the itinerary around these.

☄️

Perseid Meteor Shower Peak

Night of Wed Aug 12 → Thu Aug 13

The best Perseid year of the decade. New moon on Aug 12 = moonless sky, 90+ meteors/hour at peak (1–4 AM local). Drive 30 min to Cabo da Roca — westernmost point of mainland Europe, dark-sky bluff over the Atlantic. Bring blankets, a thermos of bica, a bottle of Setúbal Moscatel. Plan to be back at hotel by 4 AM.

Viewing guide →
🎆

Assumption Day in Cascais

Sat Aug 15 — Portuguese national holiday

Religious processions in the morning at Igreja da Assunção, then Cascais lights up for the night. 10 PM fireworks over the bay from Marina de Cascais; harbor restaurants stay open late. Hotel concierges arrange terrace tables — request one for 8 PM with a view of the marina. Trains to Lisbon run late; don't plan a Lisbon dinner this night.

🎷

Festival de Sintra · summer concerts

Runs through your trip · weekend concerts

The chamber-music + jazz festival of the Sintra hills. Concerts in 18th-century palaces (Quinta da Regaleira ballroom, Palácio Nacional) and the Centro Cultural Olga Cadaval. 30 min drive or 35 min train from Cascais. Aim for the Sat Aug 15 evening concert if you want a refined alternative to the Cascais fireworks. Tickets €25–€60.

🎨

Cascais Marina nightly summer market

Every evening in your week, 7 PM–midnight

Largo Cidade de Vitória and the marina boardwalk fill with local makers, painters, ceramicists, and street musicians from 7 PM nightly through August. Built into your post-dinner walks — wander down from any hotel, watch the sun fall behind the marina, occasionally something is worth taking home. Free.

The Glass

Two Regions, 50 Minutes Apart

The fortified Moscatels of Setúbal and the cool-climate whites of Colares — both small, both serious, and both rarely poured outside Portugal. Most visits are by appointment; details below.

Setúbal AOC · the Moscatels

Portugal's Oldest Fortified Wine

The Setúbal peninsula has produced fortified Moscatel since at least the 16th century, but the AOC was codified in 1907. The Moscatel de Setúbal and the rarer red-skin Moscatel Roxo are aged in century-old oak — the 20-year and 40-year bottlings are world-class. 50 min south of Cascais across the Tagus bridge.

José Maria da Fonseca ↗
Founded 1834, Azeitão. Vila Nogueira de Azeitão cellars. Mon–Sat tours 10–12 & 2:30–5. Tasting +$10. +351 21 219 8940.
Quinta da Bacalhôa ↗
15th-century manor + azulejo museum + cellars. Reds and Moscatels. Mon–Sat by appointment. +351 21 219 8060.
Adega Cooperativa de Palmela ↗
The local co-op — honest value, traditional Castelão reds. Walk-in welcome Sat mornings.
Casa Ermelinda Freitas ↗
Fourth-generation winemaker Leonor Freitas. Modern facility outside Setúbal. By appointment.
Colares AOC · the survivors

The Wine That Survived Phylloxera

Portugal's smallest AOC — and possibly Europe's most extreme. Ramisco vines planted directly in Atlantic dune sand (which phylloxera couldn't penetrate), 15 minutes north of Cascais on the wild Sintra coast. Less than 20 hectares left in production. Reds and whites both age for decades. The bottles are difficult to find outside Portugal.

Adega Regional de Colares ↗
The historic co-op — founded 1931, defended Colares from extinction. Tastings + cellar tour Tue–Sat. +351 21 929 1210.
Viúva Gomes ↗
Family producer continuing the dune-grown Ramisco tradition. Library going back to 1934. By appointment.
Casal Santa Maria ↗
Estate on the Penedo cliff with sea-view tasting room. Reds, whites, and Bual aperitif. Daily 11–6.
Quinta de Parato ↗
Sub-region of Lisbon AOC, 30 min north. Modern bottlings of Castelão and Touriga. By appointment.
