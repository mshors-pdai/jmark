Perseid Meteor Shower Peak · Cabo da Roca

Night of Wed Aug 12 → Thu Aug 13 · arrival night · moonless

The decade's best Perseid year — new moon Aug 12, dark sky, ~90 meteors/hr. Peak rates 02:00–04:00 GMT (= local Portugal). Cabo da Roca is THE vantage — westernmost point of continental Europe, cliff-top park, 140-m drop straight to Atlantic, certified-dark-sky country. Drive 25 min from Guincho, park near the lighthouse, walk to the south bluff. Bring blankets, a thermos of bica, a bottle of Setúbal Moscatel. Alternative: book a private cliff-edge dinner at the Fortaleza terrace and watch from your hotel — the 17th-c fortress sits on an unlit headland, same dark sky.

Cascais Maritime Procession · Senhora dos Navegantes

Sat Aug 15 — Assumption Day public holiday · fishermen's procession by sea

The annual Cascais fishermen's procession honoring Our Lady of the Navigators. Mass at the parish church, then the statue is carried in procession down to the wharf, loaded onto fishing boats, and rowed out to the Ermida da Guia (the small chapel on the coast road toward Boca do Inferno). Flowers thrown into the sea. Hundreds of locals in the boats; thousands line the Paredão promenade. The most authentic single moment of the week — and it happens before the Van Helden show, so you can do both in one Saturday.

Setúbal Peninsula · the Moscatels

Portugal's Oldest Fortified Wine

The Setúbal peninsula has produced fortified Moscatel since the 16th century; the AOC was codified in 1907. The Moscatel de Setúbal and the rarer red-skin Moscatel Roxo age in century-old oak — 20-year and 40-year bottlings are world-class. The Periquita reds (Castelão grape) are Portugal's best-selling table reds. 50 min south of Cascais via the Tagus bridge.

Adversarial honesty — where Cascais loses (and where it wins)

vs. San Sebastián: SS wins the food math — 3 × ★★★ in 12 mi (Arzak, Akelarre, Martín Berasategui) + Aste Nagusia's 8 nights of fireworks Aug 14–22. Lisbon has zero ★★★. SS pintxos breadth is in another league. BUT Joann-seafood is far worse in SS (Basque cuisine is fish-anchored). And SS lacks Cascais's water-sport breadth + the Perseids at Cabo da Roca + the only US nonstop.

vs. Mallorca: Mallorca wins the trophy hotel + restaurant combination — Belmond La Residencia in Deià + Voro ★★ at Park Hyatt Canyamel + Maca de Castro ★★. Mallorca's calmer Med swimming is real (Cascais has Guincho currents). Mallorca's wine catalog (70+ producers across 2 DOs) is wider. BUT Cascais has 3 × ★★ in Lisbon + the Cabo da Roca Perseids + only US nonstop + better Aug 15 alignment + cheaper.

vs. Côte d'Azur: Côte d'Azur wins on Michelin density (Mirazur ★★★ + many ★★) + glamour nightlife (Caves du Roy, Jimmy'z, Les Caves du Roy at Tropez). Higher hotel ceiling (Cap-Eden-Roc, La Réserve Ramatuelle, Cap d'Antibes). BUT Cascais wins on cost (~60% cheaper for equivalent week) + on the Perseids new-moon vantage + on the only US nonstop + on activity-density breadth.

Other weaknesses: (1) Cascais nightlife after midnight is meaningfully quieter than Lisbon's — most real late requires a 40-min train. The 3+2 split mitigates this. (2) Guincho's Nortada (20–35 knots Aug) is brutal for beach lounging — Cascais's central Praia da Rainha + Praia da Conceição are sheltered alternatives. (3) Joann-seafood is the soft spot — flag at every ★ booking, the kitchens accommodate. (4) Aug 15 = Assumption Day public holiday → banks + non-tourist shops shut, tourist restaurants packed (book 60 days for any Sat dinner). (5) August Sintra is mobbed — pre-book Pena 09:30 timed slot or skip. (6) Perseids viewing depends on Wed Aug 12 weather; cloud cover >60% kills it. Fortaleza Guincho terrace is the moonless-dark-cape backup.
