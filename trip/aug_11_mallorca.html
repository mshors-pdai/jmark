The Itinerary

Only This Week

Don't-Miss Events on Your Exact Dates

Four things that only happen during the week you're there. The Perseid + Cap de Formentor combination is the trip's unrepeatable night.

☄️

Perseid Meteor Shower Peak

Night of Wed Aug 12 → Thu Aug 13

The best Perseid year of the decade. New moon on Aug 12 = moonless sky, 90+ meteors/hour at peak (1–4 AM local). Mallorca has the rare combination of dark sky over the sea and a road-to-the-lighthouse to enable it: Cap de Formentor, the island's NE tip, has minimal light pollution and an open lighthouse promontory. Drive up after 10 PM (the daytime closure ends), stake out the lighthouse parking, watch over the Mediterranean horizon. Drive back to Deià by 4:30 AM. Bring blankets, thermoses, a bottle of cava.

⛪

Assumption of Mary · Mare de Déu

Sat Aug 15 — national public holiday

Spain's biggest summer holiday. Religious processions and "Els Llits de la Mare de Déu" (Virgin Mary recumbent displays) in churches across Palma and many villages — Biniaraix, Caimari, Valldemossa, Sineu, Esporles, Sa Coma, Son Serra de Marina, Colònia de Sant Pere. Cultural fairs, traditional music and dance, children's activities. Restaurants packed, beaches full, coastal roads gridlocked. Stay local in Deià + Valldemossa this day; don't try Sa Calobra or Palma. Book Sat dinner 60+ days ahead.

August events guide →
🚗

Sa Calobra Crowd Window

Best before 8:30 AM or after 5:30 PM

The 10 km road of hairpins ending at the Torrent de Pareis gorge is on every Mallorca shortlist — and in August, every tour bus on the island also runs it. Be at the trailhead by 7 AM, swim by 8, drive back out before the buses arrive (around 10). Alternative: arrive at 5 PM for the soft late-afternoon light and the buses gone. Don't attempt 11 AM–4 PM — the road is single-lane in places and the wait for oncoming buses can be 30+ minutes.

🚦

Cap de Formentor · Daytime Road Closure

Closed 10 AM → 10 PM, June 1 – Sept 30

The lighthouse road is closed to private cars in summer — you must take the public bus from Playa de Formentor parking, OR drive up before 10 AM, OR after 10 PM. For the Perseids viewing, the 10 PM reopening is ideal. For a sunset visit, you'll need the bus (~30 min from parking) and accept you can't drive back yourself until 10 PM. Plan the day around this: morning at Cap de Formentor before 10 AM, then drive south through Pollença + Alcúdia for lunch.

🍴 Bonus — Voro ★★ in Canyamel (the ambitious add-on)

Voro is Mallorca's only 2-star — Chef Álvaro Salazar at the Park Hyatt Mallorca in Canyamel (NE coast). Two tasting menus: "Voro" 15–18 courses €165, "Devoro" 20–22 courses €195. From Deià it's 1h 45m each way — too far for a casual dinner. Two ways to make it work: (1) lunch on a day you're doing Sa Calobra + Cap de Formentor (geographically adjacent — combine the day's drive) or (2) overnight at the Park Hyatt itself (would break the single-base plan; only if Voro is truly the trip-maker). Book early — peak season slots go 60+ days out. Email voro.parkhyattmallorca@hyatt.com or call +34 871 811 234.

The Glass

Two DOs, 30 Minutes Apart

Mallorca has two official Denominations of Origin: DO Binissalem (between Inca and Palma) and DO Pla i Llevant (central + eastern, around Manacor and Felanitx). Small but real — most don't export at scale, so the bottles you taste here are bottles you can only really drink here. The signature reds are made from Manto Negro; the whites from Prensal Blanc and Moll.

DO Binissalem · the heritage zone

Mallorca's First DO (1991)

The older + larger of the two DOs, between Inca and Palma. Manto Negro dominates the reds (medium-bodied, savory, lower acidity); Prensal Blanc the whites (citrus, herbal, food-friendly). Closer to Deià (~30 min via Ma-13) — easier wine-day target than Pla i Llevant.

Beyond the Table

Things You Won't Do Anywhere Else

The Tramuntana is UNESCO-listed for its 1,000-year-old dry-stone landscape. Sa Calobra and Cap de Formentor are the iconic drives. The Sóller train is a Spain rarity. Pick three or four.

🥾

GR-221 Dry Stone Route

The 140 km UNESCO trail. Best day section from Deià: Cami des Pintors to Sóller (7 km, easy, Arab path). Ambitious: Valldemossa→Sóller (24 km, 1,200 m gain, "most rewarding").

🚗

Sa Calobra Hairpin Drive

10 km of relentless hairpins ending at the Torrent de Pareis gorge beach. Go at 7 AM to avoid the tour-bus convoys. The "Nus de Sa Corbata" knot photo stop is on the way down. Swim in vertical-walled rock canyon.

🚌

Cap de Formentor Lighthouse

Mallorca's NE tip. Road closed to cars 10 AM–10 PM in summer — take the public bus from Playa de Formentor parking. Drive yourself for the Perseids (after 10 PM) or before 10 AM for an early-morning visit.

🚃

1912 Sóller Train + Tram

Vintage narrow-gauge railway Palma→Sóller (1 h, through Tramuntana tunnels) + 1912 wooden tram Sóller→Port de Sóller. Operating ~10 round trips/day in summer. ~$30 RT for the train, $10 for the tram.

🏊

Cala Deià Swim

Steep 25-minute walk down from Deià village to a pebble cove with two cliff-restaurants (Ca's Patró March + Sa Cala). Crystal water, jump rocks, rope swing. The best after-hike swim. Walk back up at sunset.

⛪

Valldemossa Cartoixa

The Carthusian monastery where Chopin and George Sand wintered in 1838–39. Walk Chopin's cell, see the original Pleyel pianoforte. Pair with coca de patata pastries at Ca'n Molinas.

⛵

Port de Sóller Boat Day

Charter a small RIB or take a sunset cruise from Port de Sóller harbor. Coastline-only — the NW coast cliffs are otherwise inaccessible by land. Sa Calobra approachable by sea instead of road.

🏰

Palma Cathedral (La Seu)

13th–16th-century Gothic cathedral with Gaudí + Miquel Barceló interventions. Best at 3:30 PM in summer when the rose-window light hits the floor. Pair with old-town walk + Es Baluard contemporary art museum.

Honest read: Mallorca Standard is the most-expensive Standard of the six options ($17,050 vs. Provence baseline $9,580) — almost entirely because Belmond La Residencia peak nights are 2–3× more than the Provence/Cascais/Basque/Madeira marquee hotels.
