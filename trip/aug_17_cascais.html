  • 3:00 PM Walk Chiado — through Largo do Carmo to Convento do Carmo (open-roof Gothic ruin, the literal centerpiece of the 1755 earthquake story). Down the Santa Justa Lift (the wrought-iron Eiffel-trained engineer landmark, 1902).
  • 5:30 PM Sunset cocktail at TOPO Chiado (rooftop on Rua do Carmo, daily 12:30–02:00, 360° rooftop view; PARK rooftop is closed Mon — TOPO is the Mon sunset). Or refresh at the hotel.
  • 7:30 PM Aperitif at the Bairro Alto Hotel rooftop BAHR & Terrace bar (Tagus + Castelo de São Jorge view across the river)
  • 8:30 PM Dinner at Bairro do Avillez · Taberna — Avillez's casual Bairro Alto gastropub (Rua Nova da Trindade, noon–midnight), Iberian snacks, presunto board, the kitchen still warm at 22:00. Joann: cured-meat board + dry-aged steak. Alternative: Solar dos Presuntos (since 1974), the Lisbon meat anchor — suckling pig leitão, lamb, presunto bar.

  • 12:00 AM Mesa de Frades late fado seating · 18th-c restored chapel, possibly Lisbon's oldest continuous fado venue, azulejo-tiled walls floor-to-ceiling. Mon–Sat 20:00–02:30; the 00:00–02:00 late set is the post-dinner move. Share a Douro red, eat the alheira sausage if you didn't hit the full Bairro do Avillez dinner. +351 917 029 436 to book 2–3 weeks ahead.

Setúbal Peninsula · the Moscatels

Portugal's Oldest Fortified Wine

The Setúbal peninsula has produced fortified Moscatel since the 16th century; the AOC was codified in 1907. The Moscatel de Setúbal and the rarer red-skin Moscatel Roxo age in century-old oak — 20-year and 40-year bottlings are world-class. The Periquita reds (Castelão grape) are Portugal's best-selling table reds. 50 min south of Cascais via the Tagus bridge.

Colares AOC · the phylloxera survivors

The Sand-Vineyard Wine That Survived Phylloxera

Portugal's smallest AOC — and Europe's most extreme. Ramisco vines planted directly in Atlantic dune sand (phylloxera couldn't penetrate), 15 min north of Cascais on the wild Sintra coast. Less than 20 hectares left in production — the AOC was 1,500 ha at its peak in the 1930s, now 1% of that. Unique in Europe — only place ungrafted vinifera vines still produce commercial wine. Reds and Malvasia whites both age for decades.
