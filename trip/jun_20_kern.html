When to Go

Three candidate dates, side-by-side

Kernville's window is narrow. Earlier than June the water's too cold; later than June the river runs dry while the valley runs hot. Comparison below uses 5-year NOAA historical data (2020–2024, ±3 days around each date) and the USGS Kern-at-Kernville flow record.

Verdict — go June 20

Of the three candidates, Jun 20 has by far the lowest heat risk (23% vs 46% for July, 31% for August) and the highest probability of running water. Mid-July hits triple digits roughly half the time; early August is usually below rafting flow. * Flow is highly snowpack-dependent — call Sierra South (760-376-3745) in early June to confirm Lickity Split is running on Jun 20, and pull the USGS gauge one week before to verify.

Booking Pre-Checks

Before you put money down

Four things to verify before locking in the date. Skip any of them and you risk a wasted weekend.

🎯

Target weekend: June 13–14

Second weekend · best balance of flow + heat

Snowpack still feeding the Lickity Split section reliably; valley temperatures in the mid-80s to low-90s; nights cool. Backup options: Jun 6–7 (slightly colder water) or Jun 20–21 (slightly hotter). After June 21, conditions degrade fast.

NWS Kernville forecast ↗
💧

Check the flow one week before

USGS gauge · "Kern River at Kernville"

If flow is > 1,500 cfs, call the outfitter and confirm Lickity Split is still running family trips. If they hesitate, postpone a week. Below 600 cfs is too low. Sweet spot: 700–1,400 cfs.

USGS gauge ↗
🌲

Confirm Sherman Pass is open

Sequoia NF · Kern River Ranger District

Sherman Saddle (9,200 ft) holds snow into mid-June in big snow years. Call the ranger district two days before the trip to confirm the road is open to the saddle. If it's not, drive as high as conditions allow and turn around — the lower half is still spectacular.

760-376-3781 ↗
🌡️

Heat-bail rule

If forecast > 100°F · postpone

The whole plan falls apart over 100°F — the kids will be miserable, the truck's range plummets, and the lodge's pool turns into a holding pen. If the 10-day forecast shows triple digits, just push the trip a week. The whole window is forgiving.

⚠ If Sherman Pass is snowed in

Swap in the Cherry Hill / Greenhorn Mountain loop (Route 2 below) — shorter and lower-elevation, gives you a relaxed morning at the lodge and an earlier start home. Same scenic-dirt-road feel, no snow risk.

The Routes

Three routes you'd actually take. Three you wouldn't.

The R1T is wide (87 in) and heavy (~7,000 lbs). That rules out tight rocky trails — and frees you up for some of the best scenic dirt roads in Southern California. Picked for beginner-friendly + family-friendly.

Route 01 · Primary

Sherman Pass Road · 22S05

The route. A wide, well-maintained Forest Service road that climbs from the Kern valley to over 9,000 feet, with views of Mount Whitney from the saddle. Zero technical sections. Confidence-building from start to finish.

SurfaceGraded dirt
Distance~50 mi RT
Time4–6 hrs
Difficulty1 / 5
  • Highlights: Sherman Pass Vista (east view to Mt. Whitney), Bald Mountain Lookout spur, Big Meadow picnic stop.
  • Elevation gain: 2,600 ft → 9,200 ft. Bring layers — easily 40°F at the saddle with wind.
  • R1T: All-Terrain mode the whole way. Drop to ~30 PSI for washboard comfort; no need to fully air down.
  • Energy: ~30–40% of pack for the round trip due to climb. Don't start with less than 75%.

The backup. If Sherman Pass is closed for snow, or if the family wants something shorter and lower-elevation, this is the alternative. Quiet pine-forest cruise, very low traffic. Less dramatic than Sherman Pass — more pastoral.

⚠ Routes to skip on this trip

Jawbone Canyon OHV — sandy + technical, designed for dirt bikes and narrow OHVs. Piute Mountains technical trails — rock crawling, not for a stock R1T or a beginner. Anything rated 3+ on the OHV scale, anything described as "rock crawling" or "tight." And don't trust the in-truck nav for Forest Service roads — pre-download onX Offroad or Gaia GPS for offline use, and pick up a paper Sequoia NF Kern River District map at the ranger station.

The River

One trip. Don't get talked into more.

The Lickity Split is exactly the trip you want — Class II–III, ~1 hour on the water, minimum age 6, guide in every boat. Three outfitters in Kernville run it; all three are reliable. Don't let anyone upsell you to the Powerhouse Run with a 7-year-old aboard.

The Truck

R1T technical notes

The truck is more capable than the driver, the driver is more capable than the trail, and that's the right order. Here's the specific drive-mode + tire + charging plan.

Drive modes & tire PSI

All-Terrain, 25 PSI, layers in the frunk

Set the truck to All-Terrain when you leave pavement — it raises the suspension and softens throttle response. Leave it there for the whole off-road portion. Don't use Rally mode with kids and a nervous spouse in the truck. On dirt, drop tires to ~25 PSI for comfort and traction. Re-air at the Mobil station on Sierra Way before driving home.

Charging plan

~450 mi round trip + ~80 mi off-road

Pre-load PlugShare, EVgo, and ChargePoint apps; test payment on each one a week before the trip — don't do this for the first time at 6 AM in a parking lot.

⚠ Don't trust the in-truck nav for Forest Service roads

Pre-download Gaia GPS or onX Offroad for the Kern River area before leaving home (cell service is dead north of Kernville). Pick up a paper Sequoia NF Kern River District map at the ranger station for a few dollars — it doesn't run out of battery and it's the source of truth for road closures.

What to Bring

Pre-staged bags, no morning-of scrambling

Two scenarios — wet (river) and high (mountain) — need different gear. Pack them separately and label.

On the river

  • Quick-dry shirt + shorts (each)
  • Swimsuit worn underneath
  • Water sandals or old sneakers
  • Reef-safe sunscreen SPF 50+
  • Sunglasses w/ retainer strap
  • Hat w/ chin strap
  • Towel per person
  • Full change of clothes in truck
  • Plastic bag for wet swimwear

On the mountain

  • Layers — fleece or light puffy
  • Long pants
  • Closed-toe shoes
  • Picnic lunch + snacks
  • 1 gallon water per person
  • Thermos (coffee, cocoa)
  • Paper Sequoia NF map
  • Trash bag (pack it out)
  • Toilet paper + small trowel
  • Basic first aid kit

The truck

  • Tire deflator
  • Portable inflator
  • Tire plug kit
  • Jumper pack (12V)
  • Recovery boards (insurance)
  • Full charge cable bag
  • EVgo + ChargePoint apps
  • Offline maps downloaded
  • Printed paper directions

The kids

  • Books / audiobooks downloaded
  • Deck of cards, small games
  • Motion sickness chewables
  • Familiar snacks (don't experiment)
  • Comfort item per child
  • Headphones each
  • Refillable water bottle each
